# Tavi: eager porter, useful companion

Performance direction, 4 October 2026. The approved coral paper suitcase and holiday theatre stay. This revision follows feedback that Tavi should roll onto the screen and feel more enthusiastic; the prolonged half-lidded expression in the earlier motion study read as bored.

## Personality

Tavi is delighted about the holiday and quietly excellent at the paperwork. He is the helpful porter who arrives a little too eagerly, catches himself, then gets everything into order. His humour lives in timing, physical paper mechanics and small recoveries.

The default face is awake and attentive: open cream-and-navy eyes, readable pupils, relaxed lifted brows and a warm smile. Brief surprise, concentration and pride vary the performance. Avoid using a sustained squint or deadpan eyelids as shorthand for sophistication. A blink is a quick punctuation mark, not a resting expression.

## The entrance and reveal

The concept film uses two generated shots joined into one preview. Keep the two moments separate in the interactive app:

1. **Arrival.** Empty stage. Tavi rolls in from the left on visibly turning wheels, brakes a little too eagerly, rocks back and recovers. The tag flutters once. He looks towards the traveller with a bright smile and an inviting hand. The destination control can already be usable while this happens.
2. **Anticipation.** Once the destination is chosen, he makes a small eager preparatory tilt and reaches for the lid. His eyes and raised brows suggest he cannot wait to show what is inside.
3. **Discovery.** The lid opens; his mouth briefly becomes a delighted little O. As paper scenery unfolds, his eyes follow one rising feature, then return to the traveller. Preserve the front-panel face and grounded wheels.
4. **Pride.** He presents the finished world with open arms and a satisfied small nod: “look what we made.” Hold a warm attentive pose rather than a frozen grin or prolonged bored look.

The generated entrance is four seconds and the reveal eight seconds. Those lengths allow assessment of the acting and mechanics. They are not required shipping waits. The finished character rig should shorten, blend and interrupt actions at the traveller's pace. The arrival is first-run theatre; retrieving a ticket later should be immediate.

## Behaviour follows the task

| Situation | Tavi's acting | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| First greeting | Brisk entrance, tiny brake recovery, open-eyed smile | Welcomes and establishes a helpful character |
| Destination chosen | Eager tilt, glance towards lid, surprised delight | Shares anticipation of this holiday |
| Dates confirmed | One proud stamp; tag settles into place | Makes the real departure tangible |
| Reading a booking | Calm attentive gaze; tidy one loose paper | Shows work in progress without inventing progress |
| Checking details | Rest pose with open attentive eyes | Gives accurate review the space it needs |
| Successful save | Straighten a slightly wonky ticket, satisfied nod | Marks a real completed operation |
| Group preview | Extend the same plan towards companions | Demonstrates sharing clearly |
| Import fails | Stop the playful performance; return the source calmly | Supports recovery with useful explanation |
| Offering a pass | Warm, measured gesture towards two stable tickets | Leaves prices and scope easy to understand |
| Continue free | Same warm folio handover and small wave | Preserves a welcome independent of spending |

Use explicit app events to select these states. Do not infer emotions from personal data or require extra onboarding questions to drive the character.

## Humour repertoire

Start with a handful of authored beats. Each is short and connected to something Tavi is doing:

- Arrives slightly too fast, brakes, and regains his composure with a grin.
- A calendar leaf sticks to his stamping hand; one small shake frees it.
- A ticket emerges a little crooked; he carefully squares it in the folio.
- A paper plane passes his handle; he ducks, then catches its tail.
- He proudly extends one long shared itinerary, then realises he needs both hands to hold it.

Play only one gag around a major action. Reading a time, correcting an import and comparing prices receive a calm stage. Optional discovered interactions can repeat on request; routine operations should not repeatedly make the traveller wait for the same joke.

## Voice

Short, friendly and useful. He speaks like someone excited to help:

- “Where are we off to?”
- “Oh, this is going to be good.”
- “When do we leave?”
- “Paperwork. My speciality.”
- “There. One less thing to juggle.”

Use text first. The current films are silent. A future sound layer can add wheel ticks, the clasp, paper flutter and the stamp; keep it optional. Avoid continuous narration or chatter while people review their own travel information.

## Generated studies and production

The new studies use edited imagegen start/end artwork and the local Scene Studio API. Scene Studio forwards the request to its configured Seedance 2.0 provider on Replicate, then downloads the MP4 into its media library. Rendering is remote; storage and the control app are local. The configured Replicate account funds renders. The local generator's source and settings were not changed.

The exact prompts and requests are in [onboarding-v3/PROMPTS.md](mockups/onboarding-v3/PROMPTS.md). The more enthusiastic closed and open poses are performance references, not replacements for every expression. For production, retain separate brows, pupils, mouth poses, arms, lid, tag and wheels in the rig so these states can be authored reliably and interrupted safely.

Check generated takes for the requested wheeled entrance, identity consistency, eye openness, variation in expression, grounded wheels, arm count, unobscured face and continuity between clips. Keep the earlier studies for comparison. Update the selected take only after visual review.
