Why family holidays need a different kind of app

Most itinerary apps are built for one traveller organising their own flights. A family holiday is different: one person usually books, four or five people live it, and at least one of them is asking "what are we doing today?" at 7am. The plan has to work for the organiser, the other adult, and the grandparents who booked their own flights, on whatever phones they have.

Travary was designed around that. It doesn't ask anyone to build a trip first or learn a planning tool. You add what you've booked, and the holiday arranges itself into days.

Everyone on the same plan

With a Holiday Pass ([[price_pass]] for one holiday) or the Annual Pass ([[price_annual]] a year), you can share a trip with up to six people, you included, by sending them a short code. Everyone sees the same days on their own phone. Each person can add what they booked, so when the grandparents book their own flights, they add them, and the plan stays whole without anyone forwarding emails.

Passports are the exception. Travel documents never leave each person's phone and are never shared with the trip, even inside a family.

Built for the day itself

[[screen|today_orlando|Travary's Today screen on day 3 of a family trip to Orlando: a theme park ticket next up, the hotel's key card, lunch and dinner on a time rail]]

The Today screen is the heart of it. During the holiday, the app opens on today: the greeting, then the day's cards down a time rail. The theme park ticket sits in its place in time with "Show tickets" on it, the hotel's key card comes after, then lunch and dinner. Finished things fold away under "Earlier", so the screen always answers the question "what's next?"

It works with no signal. The plan and the tickets are stored on the phone, which matters at the park gate, the ferry port and the airport, where signal tends to disappear.

Small things that help with children

  • Times exactly as printed. A dinner at 6:30pm stays 6:30pm whatever time zone your phone was in when you booked it, so nobody turns up an hour early.
  • Gaps spotted for you. A night without anywhere to stay, or a missing flight home, shows up as a dashed row in the trip before it becomes a problem.
  • A countdown children understand. A postcard and a luggage tag with the number of days, which is easier than "it's in March".
  • Quick to add. Only a title and a date are required. Or pick a screenshot or PDF of the confirmation and Smart Import reads it for you to check ([[imports]] free, unlimited with a pass).

What it doesn't do (yet)

Travary isn't a research tool: it doesn't suggest attractions or draw your route on a map. It doesn't send flight delay alerts yet. If you want to research a trip with a map, use a planner such as Wanderlog alongside it, then put what you actually book into Travary. More in the best itinerary apps, compared.

What it costs a family

Everything for your own trips is free: unlimited trips and bookings, Today, the countdown, tickets offline and travel documents. Family sharing needs a pass: a Holiday Pass covers one trip of up to 30 days with a single payment, not a subscription; the Annual Pass covers every trip you organise for a year. A pass on a trip covers everyone on it. Prices are in US dollars; the stores show your local price.
