What Travary does for a Disney trip

A Disney holiday is the most booked holiday most families ever take: flights, a resort, park tickets, a dozen dining reservations made months ahead, a day at Universal, a car, maybe Kennedy Space Center. Disney's own app is essential inside the parks. What it doesn't hold is the rest of the holiday, in one place, for everyone.

Each park day, in order

On a park day, Travary opens on that day: the park as the featured ticket at the top of the time rail, your resort's key card after it, then the day's dining reservations in time order. Add Lightning Lane return times or a show as their own entries if you like having everything on one rail. Finished things fold under "Earlier" as the day goes on.

Every time stays exactly as printed on the confirmation, in Florida time, so a family flying in from abroad sees a 6:30pm dinner as 6:30pm, whatever time zone their phones were in when they booked it.

The countdown, done properly

Disney holidays are booked a long way out, and the countdown is half the fun. Between trips, Travary's Today screen becomes a postcard from Orlando with a luggage tag counting the days. It knows the date because it knows your bookings, so it's never out of date. Want one now? The free holiday countdown on an Orlando postcard works in any browser.

For the whole family

  • Everyone on one plan: share the trip with up to six people with a Holiday Pass, on iPhone and Android, and each adult has the tickets on their own phone.
  • The grandparents' flights: they add their own bookings to the shared trip.
  • Passports and ESTAs for international visitors: keep travel documents on each phone behind Face ID, with passports checked against the trip.
  • A "Before you go" list: for the jobs with deadlines, like the day dining reservations open.
