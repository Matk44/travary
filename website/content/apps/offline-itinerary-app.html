Why offline matters more than anything else

The moments you need your itinerary are the moments signal disappears: the airport gate, a train in a tunnel, the ferry, the queue for the park, the first hour in a new country before your roaming kicks in. An itinerary that lives in the cloud, or a ticket that's only a link in an email, fails exactly then.

Many travel apps put offline access behind a subscription. Travary doesn't: it's how the app works, on every plan.

How to make sure your trip is ready offline

  1. Add the tickets, not just the details

    Attach the PDF, screenshot or boarding pass to each booking. The file is stored on your phone with it.

  2. Open the trip once before you leave

    On a shared trip, opening it with signal downloads the tickets the others added.

  3. Test it

    Turn on aeroplane mode and open today's plan and a ticket. If it opens now, it'll open at the gate.

  4. Keep boarding passes in your wallet too

    Airlines' own passes in Apple Wallet or Google Wallet also work offline, and are a good backup. More in do I need to print my boarding pass?

Times that don't shift when you land

Offline isn't the only thing that goes wrong abroad. When your phone switches time zone, calendar apps move your events, and a 7:30pm dinner can suddenly read 6:30pm. Travary keeps every time exactly as printed on the ticket, in the local time where it happens, with no time zone attached, so the plan and the clock agree when you arrive. (Why flight times work this way.)

What Travary doesn't do offline

Travary doesn't include maps. For getting around without data, download the area in Google Maps or another offline map app before you go, and keep Travary for the plan and the tickets.
