Flying home on day 8. If you have fewer days, drop day 6 first, then day 7. If you have more, add another rest day before you add another park.

Before you book

  • Tickets. Multi-day theme park tickets cost less per day than single days, so decide how many park days you really want first. Four is plenty for most families with young children.
  • Skipping queues. Disney and Universal both sell paid options for shorter waits on some rides. The rules and prices change, so check the current options when you buy tickets, and decide whether you need them for one day or every day.
  • Where to stay. Disney's own hotels come with transport to the parks and early entry on some days. Villas and hotels off-site, around Kissimmee and International Drive, are usually cheaper and more spacious, but you'll want a car.
  • The weather. Summer is hot and humid, with short, heavy thunderstorms most afternoons. Spring and late autumn are kinder. Whatever the month, plan the big things for the morning.

Day by day

Day1

Arrive and settle in

Nothing booked but the journey

  1. 2:30pm
    Land at Orlando International

    Times on your ticket are local, so this is what the clocks at the airport will say. Allow an hour for immigration if you're arriving from abroad.

  2. 4:00pm
    Pick up the car, or the transfer

    Child seats booked with the car. Collect on the arrival date, not the departure date.

  3. 5:00pm
    Supermarket stop

    Water, breakfast, snacks for the park bags and sun cream.

  4. Eve
    Pool, pizza, bed

    The children will want to go to a park tonight. Don't. Day 2 starts early.

Day2

Magic Kingdom

The big one, done in two halves

  1. 7:45am
    Get to the gates before opening

    The first hour has the shortest queues of the day. Breakfast before you leave.

    Rope drop beats any skip-the-line pass
  2. 9:00am
    Fantasyland first

    The rides for younger children have the longest queues later. Do them now, while the big kids' rides are quiet too.

  3. 12:00pm
    Early lunch, then back to the hotel

    The hottest, busiest hours. A nap and a swim now saves the evening.

  4. 5:00pm
    Back for the evening

    Shorter queues as day visitors leave. Stay for the fireworks if everyone's up to it; leave before the crowd if not.

Day3

A day off

Pool, then Disney Springs

  1. Morn
    Lie-in and the pool

    The day that makes the rest of the week work. Nothing booked before lunch.

  2. 4:00pm
    Disney Springs

    Free to enter: shops, live music, the boats and plenty of places to eat. Good for a gentle evening out.

  3. 6:00pm
    Dinner, booked

    Book a table for the middle of the week so there's one evening you don't have to think about food.

Day4

Animal Kingdom

Early start, early finish

  1. 7:45am
    At the gates before opening

    The animals are most active in the cool of the morning.

  2. 8:30am
    The safari first

    An open-sided truck ride through the savannah, best early on. Then the walking trails while it's still cool.

  3. 12:30pm
    Lunch and the shows

    Sit-down shows in the shade are the best way through the midday heat.

  4. Aft
    Back to the pool

    Animal Kingdom often closes earlier than the other parks, so this is an early night.

Day5

Universal

Pick the park for your children's ages

  1. 8:00am
    At the gates before opening

    Universal Orlando has Universal Studios Florida, Islands of Adventure and the newer Epic Universe, each with its own tickets. The train between the first two needs a ticket that covers both parks.

  2. 9:00am
    The headline rides

    Do the most popular first. For under-sevens, Islands of Adventure's Seuss Landing is the gentlest corner.

    Check height limits before you queue
  3. 12:30pm
    Lunch, then a break

    If you're staying nearby, go back for a swim. If not, find the shade and the shows.

  4. Eve
    The rides you missed

    Queues often shorten in the last two hours.

Day6

Slow morning, then EPCOT

Or Hollywood Studios for Toy Story fans

  1. Morn
    Pool and pancakes

    Four big days in, everyone needs a slow start.

  2. 1:00pm
    EPCOT in the afternoon

    The World Showcase's country pavilions around the lake are made for wandering and snacking. Hollywood Studios suits families who want Toy Story Land and the big rides.

  3. 6:30pm
    Dinner in a pavilion

    Book ahead for the popular ones.

  4. 9:00pm
    Fireworks over the lake

    If the children have napped. If not, there's no shame in going home.

Day7

Space, or the sea

A change of scene before you go home

  1. 8:30am
    Drive to the coast

    Kennedy Space Center's visitor complex is about an hour east of Orlando by car.

  2. 10:00am
    Kennedy Space Center

    Real rockets, a space shuttle and astronaut stories. Check the launch schedule: you might see one.

  3. 3:00pm
    Cocoa Beach

    About half an hour south. Feet in the Atlantic before the drive back.

  4. Eve
    Pack

    Check the flight home's date and time tonight, especially if it's an overnight flight.

Tomorrow: fly home. Late flight? Ask for a late check-out, or plan a last swim and a long lunch.

Tips that make the week easier

  • Be at the gates before opening, every park day. It's the single biggest difference to how many rides you do.
  • Go back to the hotel at midday. It feels like losing time. It isn't: it's how you get a good evening.
  • Carry ponchos and a spare set of clothes for the children. Summer storms are short, heavy and frequent, and water rides soak.
  • Refill water bottles all day. Heat catches children out faster than adults.
  • Write the park opening times into the plan the evening before. They change by season and by day.
