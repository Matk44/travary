Before you go

  • Book the Eiffel Tower and the Louvre for timed entry, weeks ahead in school holidays.
  • Museums are often free for children. The Louvre and many national museums don't charge under-18s, though you still need to book a time slot.
  • The metro is fast but full of stairs. With a pushchair, buses are often easier, and a lightweight buggy or a carrier beats a big pram.
  • Check closing days. The Louvre closes on Tuesdays; many other museums close on Mondays.

Day by day

Day1

The Eiffel Tower and the river

The view first, the boat after

  1. 9:30am
    Eiffel Tower

    Booked time slot. Older children love taking the stairs to the second floor; the lift goes on to the top.

    Book weeks ahead in the holidays
  2. 12:00pm
    Picnic on the Champ de Mars

    A baguette, cheese and fruit from a shop nearby, on the grass under the tower.

  3. 2:00pm
    A boat on the Seine

    An hour-long cruise from near the tower or the Pont de l'Alma passes most of the city's sights with no walking at all. The hop-on, hop-off river bus is good for tired legs.

  4. 5:00pm
    Trocadéro

    The classic view of the tower from across the river, and the fountains below.

Day2

The Louvre and two gardens

Not on a Tuesday

  1. 9:00am
    The Louvre

    Two hours is plenty with children. Pick three things, not thirty: the Egyptian mummies, the Winged Victory on the staircase, and a look at the Mona Lisa if the crowd allows.

  2. 11:30am
    Tuileries Garden

    Right outside: trampolines, a playground, and a funfair in summer.

  3. 1:00pm
    Lunch, then across the river

    A 20-minute walk over the Pont des Arts into Saint-Germain.

  4. 3:00pm
    Jardin du Luxembourg

    Rent a wooden sailboat and push it across the pond with a stick, catch the puppet show, then the big playground. The best afternoon in Paris for under-tens.

Day3

Montmartre, then the animals

Steps in the morning, dinosaurs after lunch

  1. 9:00am
    The funicular up to Sacré-Cœur

    Skip the steps: the little funicular takes an ordinary metro ticket.

  2. 9:30am
    Sacré-Cœur and the Place du Tertre

    The view over Paris from the steps, then the square where painters sell portraits. Early, before the crowds.

  3. 12:00pm
    Crêpes for lunch

    Then the metro across town.

  4. 2:00pm
    Jardin des Plantes

    The Grande Galerie de l'Évolution's parade of animals is unforgettable, and the small zoo next door is one of the oldest in the world.

    Rainy-day swap: the Cité des Sciences
Day4

Notre-Dame and the islands

Or swap the whole day for Disneyland Paris

  1. 8:30am
    Notre-Dame

    Restored and reopened in December 2024. Entry to the cathedral is free; go early to beat the queue.

  2. 10:00am
    Sainte-Chapelle

    A few minutes' walk away: a small chapel of floor-to-ceiling stained glass. Book a time slot. Best on a bright day.

  3. 12:00pm
    Lunch and ice cream on Île Saint-Louis

    The quieter island next door is famous for its ice cream.

  4. Aft
    Free afternoon

    Back to the favourite park, or a rest before the journey home.

Disneyland Paris instead? The RER A train from central Paris reaches Marne-la-Vallée–Chessy, at the park gates, in about 40 minutes. Make it a full day, and leave the islands for the morning you go home.

Paris with toddlers

Paris works well with under-fives if you plan around naps and parks rather than museums. Every neighbourhood has a playground; the big gardens have carousels; bakeries solve most hunger emergencies. Swap the Louvre for a longer afternoon at the Luxembourg, and use the river boat to see the sights in one sitting.
