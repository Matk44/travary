Who needs to see what

"Sharing the itinerary" means two different things, and they need different tools.

WhoWhat they needBest way
People on the tripEvery booking, every ticket, each day in order, offline, kept up to dateA shared itinerary app
Co-organisers (the other family, the grandparents)The same, plus the ability to add what they've bookedA shared app where everyone can add bookings
People at homeWhere you're staying, flight numbers and times, how to reach youA PDF or a short message

The options, compared

Emailing a document or PDF

Quick, works for anyone, needs no app. It's ideal for people at home. For people on the trip it's a snapshot: change a dinner booking and the PDF is wrong, and nobody knows which version they have.

A shared document or spreadsheet

Everyone sees the latest version, and anyone can edit. On a phone it's fiddly, the tickets aren't in it, and it may not open without signal unless each person set it to work offline.

A group chat

Where most family trip plans actually live. It's great for decisions and hopeless as a record: the hotel address is 400 messages up, between a photo of the dog and a debate about sun cream.

A shared itinerary app

Everyone has the same plan on their own phone, in date order, with the tickets attached, updated the moment anything changes. The best ones work on both iPhone and Android, so the family with one of each isn't split, and let each person add what they booked themselves.

Share with people, not with a link

A trip itinerary contains your travel dates, flight numbers and where you're staying, which together say exactly when your home is empty. Several trip apps warn about this themselves. Some sensible rules:

  • Invite named people rather than posting a public link anyone can open or forward.
  • Don't post the itinerary on social media before or during the trip. Share the photos when you're home.
  • Keep passports out of it. Travel documents shouldn't be in a shared trip folder at all. See keeping a passport copy on your phone.

Keeping everyone up to date

Most mix-ups on group trips come from one person working from an old version: the original flight time, the restaurant before it moved. Two habits fix it:

  1. One source of truth.

    Agree where the plan lives, and that the group chat is for talking, not for bookings.

  2. Whoever books it, adds it.

    When the grandparents book their own flights, they add them to the shared plan. The organiser shouldn't have to chase confirmation emails.

Time zones, when family are back home

Give people at home your times in local time, as printed on the ticket, and tell them the time difference once. "We land at 13:25 New York time, which is 18:25 for you" is clearer than leaving them to work it out. More on that in are flight times local?
