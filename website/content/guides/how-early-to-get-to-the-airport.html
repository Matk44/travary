The rule of thumb

FlightArrive at the airportAdd time for
Domestic or short-haul, hand luggage only1½–2 hours beforeEarly-morning rushes, small airports with one security lane
Domestic or short-haul, checked bags2 hours beforeSchool holiday weekends
International or long-haul3 hours beforeDocument checks, a large hub airport
With young childrenAdd 30–60 minutesPushchairs, toilet stops, the queue for the family lane

These are the figures most airlines and airports publish as guidance. Your own airline's advice for your route, usually in the booking confirmation or app, overrides them.

The deadlines that actually matter

The departure time is when the aircraft leaves the gate. Long before that, two doors close, and missing either means missing the flight:

  1. Check-in and bag drop close.

    Typically 40 to 60 minutes before departure for short-haul and 60 minutes or more for long-haul, depending on the airline and airport. After that, your bag can't travel, even if you could.

  2. The gate closes.

    Typically 15 to 30 minutes before departure. Boarding usually starts before that, and at a large airport the walk from security to a far gate can take 20 minutes on its own.

Find both times for your flight (they're in the booking terms or the airline app), then work back.

What adds time

  • Parking. Long-stay car parks often mean a shuttle bus. Add 20 to 30 minutes, more at peak times.
  • The time of day. Early-morning departures stack up: many flights leave between 6am and 8am, so security is busiest then.
  • School holiday weekends and public holidays, when queues at every stage are longest.
  • Document checks. On some international routes, the airline needs to see your passport or visa at a desk before you can board, even if you checked in online.
  • Special help. If anyone needs assistance at the airport, book it in advance and arrive with extra time.

What saves time

  • Online check-in as soon as it opens, with boarding passes on your phone. (Do you still need to print them? Usually not.)
  • Hand luggage only, which lets you skip bag drop entirely.
  • Packing liquids and laptops so they come out quickly at security, if your airport still asks for that.
  • Fast-track security or trusted-traveller programmes where they exist.

Can you be too early?

Yes. Bag drop often doesn't open until two to four hours before a flight, so arriving five hours early can mean waiting landside with your cases. If your flight is late in the day, check when your airline's desks open.

The morning-flight trick

For a 6am flight, the hard part isn't the airport, it's the alarm. Lay everything out the night before, book the taxi for a time that gets you there with the full two or three hours, and check the date: a flight at 00:05 on Saturday leaves on Friday night. More on that trap.
